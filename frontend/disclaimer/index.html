AI & Mathematical Disclaimer

MathNova checks its own work. That makes it more trustworthy than a tool that does not — it does not make it correct by definition.

Last updated 20 September 2026

What “verified” means here

When a result is marked as verified, an engine has checked it by a route independent of the one that produced it — substituting a solution back into the original equation, transforming an inverse forward again, re-multiplying a solved system by its matrix.

That is a real check and it catches real errors. What it cannot do is prove the answer is the one you wanted. A verification confirms internal consistency; it cannot know that you meant a different branch, a different domain, or a different convention.

What it does not cover

  • Your input. A correctly solved version of the wrong equation is still the wrong answer. Ambiguous notation is resolved by a parser, not by asking you what you meant.
  • Conventions. Transform definitions, constant placement and sign conventions differ between textbooks and courses. A result that disagrees with your notes may be correct under a different convention.
  • Unverified results. Where an engine cannot check something it says so. Those answers carry no verification at all.
  • Numerical error. Numerical methods are approximations by nature. Convergence reported against a tolerance is not exactness.

The AI Assistant specifically

The assistant is a language model with tools. The calculations come from the same verified engines the Solver uses. The explanation around them is generated, and generated text can be wrong, incomplete, or confidently mistaken about what you asked.

The tool chips under an answer show which engines were called. If an answer contains mathematics but no chips, the model very likely produced it itself rather than calling an engine — treat that as unverified, and please report it.

Do not submit a MathNova answer as your own working without checking it. It is a study and calculation aid, and it is not a substitute for understanding the method, for your own working, or for your own judgement. You remain responsible for anything you hand in, publish or build on.
